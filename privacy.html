Privacy

Your calendar never leaves
your Mac.

MenuCalendar runs entirely on your device. There's no MenuCalendar server, no analytics, no accounts to sign up for. Here's the long version.

Last updated · June 4, 2026

The short version. MenuCalendar reads your calendars and shows them in the menu bar. It talks to Apple's EventKit and to Google's calendar API directly from your Mac. None of your data passes through any server we run, because we don't run any. We don't have analytics, error reporting, or an account system.

What MenuCalendar reads

MenuCalendar reads calendar event data from two places, with your permission, and only when the popover or settings window is open.

Source What it sees Where it lives
iCloud & Exchange
(EventKit) Calendars, event titles, times, locations, and attendees from accounts you've connected in macOS System Settings. Stays on your Mac. EventKit is Apple's system framework; data is read directly from the local calendar database.
Google Calendar
(OAuth) The same calendar fields, fetched from www.googleapis.com using a token you authorise. Stays on your Mac. Requests go from your Mac directly to Google's servers, never via MenuCalendar.
Your preferences Theme choice, week start, lead time for notifications, which calendars to show. Stays on your Mac in UserDefaults, like every other Mac app's settings.

Where your tokens live

When you connect a Google account, Google issues two tokens: an access token (short-lived) and a refresh token (long-lived). MenuCalendar stores the refresh token in the macOS Keychain — the same encrypted store macOS uses for your Wi-Fi password, Safari logins, and other app credentials. The Keychain is encrypted at rest and unlocked with your login password.

MenuCalendar never transmits these tokens anywhere except back to Google when refreshing access. If you remove the Google account from MenuCalendar's Settings, the token is deleted from the Keychain immediately.

What MenuCalendar doesn't do

No analytics.
No first-party tracking, no third-party SDKs, no crash reports, no telemetry of any kind.
No servers.
There is no MenuCalendar backend. Nothing to leak, nothing to subpoena, nothing to misconfigure.
No accounts.
There's nothing to sign up for, no email to give, no password to remember. You just download and run it.
No ads.
MenuCalendar is free because it's small and personal — not because you're the product. No upsells, no "Pro" tier, no nudges.

Permissions you control

  • Calendar access — granted via the standard macOS prompt the first time you open MenuCalendar. Revoke at any time in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars.
  • Notifications — granted via the standard macOS prompt. Revoke at System Settings → Notifications → MenuCalendar.
  • Google account — connect or disconnect at any time in MenuCalendar's Settings → Calendars tab. Disconnecting deletes the Keychain token immediately.

Network requests, in full

For completeness, here is every network destination MenuCalendar will contact:

  • accounts.google.com — only during initial OAuth sign-in, in your default browser.
  • oauth2.googleapis.com — to exchange and refresh tokens.
  • www.googleapis.com — to fetch calendars and events.
  • fonts.googleapis.com — only inside this website, not the app.

That's the entire list. iCloud and Exchange calendars are read locally via EventKit and don't generate any traffic of their own.
