First launch

Opening MenuCalendar the first time.

This is a preview build — it isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS asks you to confirm it the first time you open it. That's expected, and it's a one-time step. The signed, notarised v1.0 release will open with a single click. Until then, here's the 20-second version.

1
Unzip & move to Applications
Double-click the downloaded zip, then drag MenuCalendar.app into your Applications folder.
2
Right-click → Open
Right-click (or Control-click) the app and choose Open. The right-click menu is the trick — a plain double-click won't give you the option the first time.
3
Confirm once
A dialog says macOS can't verify the developer. Click Open. You'll only ever see this once — every launch after is normal.
› Don't see an “Open” button? (newer macOS) — or prefer Terminal

On macOS Sequoia and later, right-click → Open is sometimes blocked. Instead: try to open the app once (double-click), dismiss the warning, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to the Security section, and click Open Anyway next to the MenuCalendar message. Confirm with Touch ID or your password.

Or, from Terminal, strip the quarantine flag after moving the app to Applications:

$ xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/MenuCalendar.app

Either way it's a one-time step. None of this is needed once the notarised v1.0 ships — track it on the changelog.

Features

Everything the menu bar should already do.

Accounts
iCloud, Exchange, and Google.

EventKit pulls in everything iCloud already knows about. Google connects via OAuth — refresh tokens live in the macOS Keychain, never on a server.

Themes
Four themes, live preview.

Native, Flexoki, Minimal, Paper. Switch in Settings and watch the popover update instantly — no relaunch, no reload.

Notifications
Reminders before things start.

Local notifications fire N minutes before every timed event. Pick the lead time once; MenuCalendar quietly handles the rest.

Launch at login
There when you log in.

One toggle. Powered by SMAppService.mainApp — no helper apps, no login scripts, no surprises in System Settings.

Designed for macOS

Built native, down to the status item.

SwiftUI for the popover and Settings. AppKit where the platform expects it — the status item, the URL scheme, the lifecycle. The shipping v1.0 will be Developer ID signed and notarised by Apple; the current preview just needs one extra click the first time.

Privacy

Your calendar never leaves your Mac.

MenuCalendar talks to EventKit and to Google's servers — directly from your device. There's no MenuCalendar server. No analytics. No accounts. Google refresh tokens are stored in the macOS Keychain alongside your other credentials.
